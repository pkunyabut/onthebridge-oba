# Voice Library (EXAMPLE — replace with your publication's voice)
Version: 1.0.2 | Adapted from OnTheBridge. The line under "Words to avoid" is parsed by the code: write the avoided words on one line, separated by commas.

## Principles
Write for general readers aged 25–65+ of every gender, including readers over 65. Use a voice that reaches people at different stages of life. Never assume the reader's gender, age, parental role or family status.
Respect the reader's intelligence. Invite thought without preaching.
Start from a human being. Use verified facts. Do not use numbers to manufacture credibility in place of evidence.
Alternate short and long rhythm. Read aloud and adjust for naturalness. There is no sentence quota that has not been measured.
Use warm words (mother, kitchen, memory, taste of home) only where the context fits. Do not assume everyone has warm family memories.

## Words to avoid in the narrator's voice
shocking, exposed, must read, don't miss, ultimate, insider, best in the world, guaranteed, most urgent, secret revealed, nobody tells you, life-changing instantly, 100%, results assured
Formal words such as "utilise", "proceed", "procedure", "process" are allowed when needed, but prefer plain language first.
Stock AI connectives such as "Moreover", "However", "It can be said that" may be used at most once each per article. If not needed, cut them or use a natural connective.
A paragraph should not exceed about 4 lines (roughly 80–100 words). Longer than that, split it in two.
Direct quotes and document titles keep the original, even if they contain a word to avoid.

## Voice examples (3 pairs)
These are language examples, not real stories or factual sources. Never present them as a person's words.

1. Formal: "We should keep a record of our family's memories."
   Reflective: "What in your kitchen would you like to keep for the next generation?"
2. Too broad: "Technology changes everything."
   Human-specific: "What would you like AI to write down for you, and what would you rather tell yourself?"
3. Preachy: "Everyone must write down their recipes."
   Opens space: "If there were one recipe you wanted to keep, which would you start with?"

Before sending: read it aloud and check that no memory was invented for the reader.
Three questions before publishing (the owner answers them at the Human Gate — the AI must not answer for them):
1. Are we proud to put our name on this article?
2. If it is read again in five years, will it still have value?
3. Does it fit our motto — "know deeply, understand before you believe"?
